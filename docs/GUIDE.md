# SlotForge Foundry user guide

The full tour of SlotForge Foundry, tool by tool. For downloads and a quick introduction, see the [README](../README.md).

## Before you start

Install the desktop app from the [latest release](https://github.com/theDutchmanJames/SlotForgeFoundry/releases/latest), or use the portable app. The browser edition is in the same release: extract **SlotForge-Foundry-X.Y.Z.zip** and open **SlotForge Foundry.html**. Everything runs on your computer, without a server or an internet connection. The ZIP's **examples** folder includes a real Circuit bend in both SVG and DXF.

## Track Set

In Track Set, choose **Scale car** in the lower-right viewport toolbar. Click a road to snap the car’s guide to the nearest slot, or use **Lane** and **Position**. Compare the same 4,600 × 1,900 × 1,200 mm GT body at **1:64**, **1:43**, **1:32**, or **1:24**. The car is a visual reference and stays out of print kits.

Open a pack piece in Track Set and use the **Special pieces** shelf at the bottom of the left rail, or choose a picture tile in **Add track piece**. Choose a double-crossover hairpin, single-lane pit, flat diamond crossing, late-apex duel, bus-stop chicane or timing straight. Set the borders in the creator and refine them in Track Set. The bus stop has an overall length, lateral offset, first turn direction and independent entry and exit radii. A tighter radius needs room for the inside road shoulder and its border.

On a **Single-lane pit**, use **Road → Flipper position** to switch between the main lane and pit entry. From above, the sheltered nose rises smoothly into a blade that sits flush with the road. Choose **Bottom** to see the crank move. Select the crank to see the pocket above its pushrod eye over the full swing. With the default linkage, a pin head up to 3 mm across can project 1 mm above the tab and still have 0.4 mm of clearance.

Each pit entry comes with three printed parts for the switch. The **pivot bearing** exports with its broad face on the print bed. Glue its oblong peg into the road from above, then fit the **flipper blade**. The **actuator crank** carries the 4 mm pivot pin. Insert it from below and align the D-shaped tip with the blade. The kit’s **Assembly notes.txt** gives the M2 countersunk screw length for your track height and linkage.

Choose **Pit lane divider** when creating a single-lane pit, or enable it later under **Road → Road white lines**. The long stripe beside the pit box lines up with the original road edge. Widening the pit offset leaves the stripe there. A narrow pit can bring it inward to clear the braids. Choose **Entry and exit → Dashed** to break up the two crossing sections while keeping the line beside the box solid. **Recess road markings** cuts an outline around the painted sections, including the ends of the dashes. Set stripe width, groove width and groove depth in **Shared line and groove dimensions**.

A pack with one lane also has a **Turnout** on the Special pieces shelf. One lane comes in; a straight road and a curved branch go out, so the piece has three road ends. The branch starts on the pack’s R1 radius. Set its side, **Branch radius**, **Branch angle** and the **Lead before the branch** under **Road**. The branch must turn far enough to clear the main road; if it does not, the message gives the angle that does. A turnout prints as one road piece without borders, trackside accessories or road markings.

**A turnout in a layout.** In the Track Planner a single-lane pack has the **Turnout** under **Add a special piece**, and a turnout saved in the pack is listed with its pieces. Choose **Left** or **Right** first: that is the side the branch leaves on. The turnout goes on the selected open end with its entry, and building carries on from its main exit, end **B**. The branch is end **C** and stays open: select it under **Build from** or click it in the plan, then place the next piece there. Select a placed turnout to choose its **Insert**: Digital, Hand-set point, Analog or Closed. The road is the same for all four, so nothing moves when you change it, and with several turnouts selected the insert goes to all of them. A turnout is never cut by **Segment for printing**; if it is larger than the print plate, the check says so. Barriers, white lines and kerbs applied to a selection skip the turnouts in it.

**Building a pit lane.** Four helpers in the Track Planner do the fiddly parts.

- **A turnout splits the lane or joins two lanes.** Under the direction buttons, choose **Splits the lane** for the way into the pits and **Joins two lanes** for the way back onto the main road. A joining turnout goes in the other way round: it joins with its main exit, building carries on from its entry, and its branch reaches back on the chosen side to take the pit lane.
- **Add return curve.** Select a turnout whose branch is open and choose **Add return curve**. Corners of the chosen pack turn the branch back parallel to the main road. The pack needs a corner whose angle divides the branch angle.
- **Fit a straight.** Choose an open end under **Build from**. If another open end faces it in a straight line, **Fit a straight** makes a straight of exactly that length from the pack's straight and joins both ends. That closes a pit lane between its two return curves.
- **Pit ladder.** Builds a row of pit bays from the chosen end in one step: a turnout for each bay, the bay on its branch, and an end stop. Enter the number of bays, the spacing from one turnout to the next and the bay length. With **Bring the bays back parallel** on, each bay gets a return curve and the bays lie in one line beside the main road; off, they stand at the branch angle. The window suggests the least spacing that keeps the bays apart. One **Undo** takes the whole ladder away, and every piece can be changed afterwards.

**End stops.** An end stop is a straight whose slots and braid stop short of end B, which is closed: it has no connectors and nothing joins it. In the Track Planner it is under **Add a special piece**, made from the shortest straight of the pack. In Track Set, switch on **End stop** under **Shape** for any plain straight and set **Road beyond the slot end**. An end stop needs a slot that keeps one distance from the edge.

**Sloped pieces.** A plain straight can bend in height. Under **Track Set → Shape → Slope**, choose one of four forms. Going up: **Level into a climb** for the foot of a hill and **Climb back to level** for its top; both rise from end A to end B. Going down again: **Level into a descent** for the top and **Descent back to level** for the foot; both drop from end A to end B. A hill is those four pieces, with tilted straights between them where it is longer. **Climb angle** is the slope at the sloped end, from 1° to 30°. **Curve** is how much of the piece the bend takes: at 100 % the road bends gently over its whole length, and a smaller value bends more sharply and then runs straight at the climb angle. The hint under it gives the rise, the run and the radius of the bend, which has to be at least 150 mm for a car to follow. Every other setting works as on a level straight: the piece is made flat first and then bent, so the slots keep their depth, the braid and the connectors follow the curve, and both end faces stay square to the road. The level end joins a level piece and the sloped end a straight tilted to the climb angle. The foot of a climb or of a descent stands on the table on a **solid base** by default and is then one solid block without a hollow underside; the base stops short of the high end (**Open under the high end**) so that connectors there can be fitted from below. A sloped piece prints on its side, so the curve of the road comes out smooth. In the Track Planner it is as long as its run, and the piece after it joins at the height it reached: a straight behind a sloped end is tilted to the climb angle and carries on, up or down. A descent needs the road it joins to be at least as high as it comes down; the message says how high. A sloped piece is one road segment and cannot have trackside accessories, white lines or kerbstones yet.

The area around the split is a separate **insert**. It goes into the road from below and is held by two M2 screws, so you can swap it without lifting the braid. The road is the same for all four inserts; choose the one to print under **Road → Insert**. **Digital** has a moving blade worked by a slider and a push-pull motor. **Hand-set point** has the same blade, set by hand, with a low ridge that keeps it in either position. **Analog** has no moving parts and leaves both slots open. **Closed** fills the branch slot level with the road, so cars can only go straight on. The two braids between the slots start behind the insert and are wired from below, as shown on the conductor map in the kit.

The Digital insert is driven from the island, the wedge of road between the two slots. Nothing passes under a guide slot or a braid. A short crank on the blade pivot ends in an upright pin. The pin stands in a slanted slot in the **slider**, a stout bar that moves parallel to the main slot. A push-pull motor lies further along the island, in line with the slider, in a cradle with two screws behind it. Set **Usable stroke** to the travel of your motor, from 2 to 12 mm. From about 5 mm the slant is shallow, so the motor needs little force and the flat ends of the slot hold the blade in place; at 2 to 4 mm the slot is steep, needs more push, and a knock on the blade can push the slider back. **Pin play** is the clearance between pin and slot, 0.2 mm by default; a little play there becomes two to three times as much at the tip of the blade. Give the motor’s body size, the reach of its retracted plunger and the diameter of the eye in the plunger. The end of the slider reaches over the plunger and carries a straight pin for that eye, so no wire has to be bent. If the motor does not fit between the slots, the message says what to change. The slider prints upside down on its flat face and goes in after the insert; the crank then holds it in. The channel for the slider and the cavity for the motor are in the road whichever insert you print.

Under **Road → Pit grid marker**, enable a stopping mark on the pit slot. Move it along the straight pit bay from entry (0%) to exit (100%). The initial position is 50%. Set the width to suit the car, choose a line thickness, and adjust the leg length. Zero gives a transverse line. Longer legs form an open box. Enable the outline recess to include the paint groove in the STL. On a piece with pits on both sides, the two markers can line up or sit at different positions.

At pit entry and exit, the short braid runs can feed through the road. Open **Cross-section → Braid pass-throughs → Pit junction openings** and choose **Braid pass-throughs**. **Pit slot length** measures the opening along the braid. Its width follows **Width per strip**. The through-running braids use round wire holes underneath so each stays continuous. **Extra lane openings** adds feeds at positions you choose separately.

Switch on **Push-pull motor under the road** to drive the flipper with a rectangular push-pull motor. The motor lies under the road between the main slot and the pit slot, in line with a slider. A pin on the crank stands in a slanted slot in that slider, so moving the slider by the motor’s stroke swings the flipper, and the flat ends of the slot hold it. Nothing passes under a guide slot and no wire has to be bent. Enter the body length along the plunger, width and height, the **Retracted plunger reach** from the end face to the eye, the **Plunger eye diameter**, the **Usable stroke** (2 to 12 mm) and the **Pin play**. The live preview shows the motor outline and its plunger, and the print kit includes the slider and the slotted cradle. Use the cradle’s adjustment slots to centre the real flipper between its stops. Without the motor, the crank has an eye for a pushrod of your own that leaves at the pit side.

Special-piece print kits include individual STLs, a conductor map and assembly notes. The road and borders export driving-face down, or face up when the pack uses raised lands for copper tape. For a hairpin or diamond, use **Export crossing test STL** to try the guide and braid clearances with your cars. For a timing straight, use the bottom camera view to inspect the sensor pockets or braid feeds and recessed cable exits.

**Track Set** opens with an editable bend. Under **Create track**, choose **Track packs** or **Import Circuit**. Expand a family in **Assembly** to select or hide an individual part. The eye on a family controls all its instances. **Road** sets the path, lanes, road white lines and road segments. **Cross-section** sets the deck, guide slot and braid. Select a border in the model or parts list to open **Borders**, where its shape, kerbstones, white lines and grooves live together. **Connections** sets the hardware for both road and borders. Road and Borders each link their segment count to the print plate. The settings of each section are grouped in cards. Edit a field to see its guide and the proposed geometry in the viewport. **Apply changes** records the edit; for a piece of a pack it reads **Apply to this piece** (see [Pack pieces in Track Set](#pack-pieces-in-track-set)). **Restore** returns to the applied model, and the curved arrow beside a field restores just that value. Choose **Edit as STL** beside the selected part’s name above the model to open that part in the STL editor. Click the part name to locate its row in Assembly.

Hollow roads and borders have crosswise ribs underneath. Under **Cross-section → Road body**, use **Target rib spacing** to set the distance between supports (50 mm by default). Each rib uses the selected **Wall thickness**. Foundry distributes ribs within each printable section and adjusts them around connectors and other hardware. Choose **View underside** to inspect the layout. Turn off **Reinforce roads and borders** for an open underside, or set reinforcement for a collection under **Shared print profile**.

**Track packs → New track pack** opens a larger window with its steps in a column on the left. **Basics** is the only step you need: the pack name, what it starts with (an empty pack, a copy of the open pack, or a starter pack) and, for an empty pack, the road width, lane count, normal slot spacing, the R1 inner road radius and default sweep. The steps after it are optional and grouped as **Every piece** (**Road**, **Slot and braid**, **Connections**) and **Only if you need it** (**Borders**, **Lines and kerbs**, **Road surface**, **Trackside**). Their settings sit in folders that open and shut. Each step in the column shows what it holds now, and a green dot marks the steps you changed; the line at the bottom names them. **Next** goes to the following step, **Skip this step** leaves it as the pack starts, and **Create track pack** makes the pack from any step. A step left alone keeps what the chosen pack starts with, and everything stays editable later under **Pack rules**. **Borders** gives the plain straights and corners one border width at their ends, so the borders of two pieces meet, and a corner a wider apex in its middle; **Pack rules → Borders** has the same settings for an existing pack, and a straight or corner added later starts with them. Special pieces you already have keep their own borders. Choose **Add track piece → Curve** for a matching R1–R8 bend or an independent inner radius. Use **Straight** for a measured length and **Freeform path** for a route drawn through points. **Flowing curve / S-bend** opens the same drawing with one smooth curve in place of the points: pick **Sweeping bend**, **S-bend** or **Straight transition** under **Starting shape** and choose **Use shape**, then drag the two round handles to shape the curve and the square to move its end. Arrow keys move a selected handle 1 mm, or 10 mm with Shift. **Keep start and end directions** holds both ends square to the pieces they will join while you drag. Set **Start direction** and **End direction** in degrees, or open **Exact handle coordinates** to type positions. **Mirror to the other side** turns the curve the other way. **Preferred minimum radius** is advice only: it starts at the tightest slot of the pack, the drawing shows tighter slot sections in red, and the readout below gives the smallest radius and the slot length of each lane. A curve too tight for the road width or the lanes is refused with a note on what to move. **Shape** switches a drawing between points and a flowing curve, and **Edit drawn path** reopens a saved piece with its handles. A flowing curve has no closed loop or crossing. The creator previews the outline and slots. **Add to pack** saves the recipe and opens it in Track Set. **Road → Lane layout** lets you squeeze or spread the slots and braid shelves at mid-piece while preserving their alignment at both ends. A straight or curve can also carry its slots away from their usual places: see [Off-centre slots](#off-centre-slots).

**Trackside → Barrier side** adds concrete barriers beside the piece: outside, inside or both. A barrier stands on the table beside each border segment, or beside each road segment on a side without a border, and is as long as that edge. Its foot is as high as the track, and the sloped face rises from the road surface by **Height above the road** (20 mm to start with, 5 to 100 mm). Each barrier plugs into sockets in that edge with one or two tabs: **Tabs per barrier** is automatic (two tabs from 150 mm of edge), one or two. Barriers follow the segments of the border or road they stand beside; **Barriers per segment** cuts each of those into shorter, equal barriers, each with its own tabs, so a long edge still fits your print plate. **Barrier ends** slopes the top of the first barrier, the last barrier or both down to track height over 25 mm, for where a row of barriers stops; with **End shape → Sloped and rounded** that end also narrows to a rounded tip at the track edge. **Face angle** sets how far the sloped face leans back from upright, from 0° (a straight wall) to 45°; the measured barrier leans 17.1°, and the top always keeps at least 2 mm of flat. The tabs use the tab size and clearance under **Joints**, and all tabs of one barrier slide in along the same line. If a socket would meet a connector, a slot or a narrow border end, Foundry moves it along the edge, and says so when no place is left. A straight barrier exports on its flat back with the tabs up; a curved one exports standing and needs support under each tab in your slicer. **Export STLs → Barriers and fences only** downloads one STL per barrier and fence, and **All printable parts** includes them.

**Different barriers on each side.** With **Barrier side → Both**, switch on **Separate settings for the inside**. The settings then come twice, as **Outside barrier** and **Inside barrier**, each with its own shape, height, face angle or thickness, tabs, ends, segments and fence: an advertising board with a fence on the outside and a lower concrete barrier on the inside, for example. Switched off, both sides share one set.

**Pack rules** opens the same window of steps as a new pack, for the pack that is open: **Dimensions** (the road width, lanes and corners), **Road**, **Slot and braid** and **Connections** (the shared print profile every piece follows), **Borders**, **Lines and kerbs**, **Road surface**, **Trackside** and **Print segments**. Change any steps; a green dot marks them, and **Apply to all pieces** gives every change to every piece at once. Every piece is checked first, so a setting one piece cannot take is refused and the pack stays as it was. A piece added later starts with the pack rules.

### Pack pieces in Track Set

In Track Set the column on the left shows the open pack: the red **Pack rules** button, with a line on what the rules set, then its pieces grouped as straights, corners, drawn pieces and special pieces, and the special pieces it can still have as small **+** buttons. A green dot on a piece, and on a section in the bar above the model, marks settings of its own that differ from the pack rules (borders, lines and kerbs, road surface or trackside). That section then opens with **Use the pack rules**, which gives the piece the pack's settings back.

Two buttons apply an edit. **Apply to this piece** keeps it on this piece only. **Apply to Pack Rules** makes it the pack's: a shared setting (road, slot and braid, connections, road width or lanes) goes to every piece, and so do borders, lines and kerbs, road surface and trackside as the new pack rules. With no edit waiting, **Apply to Pack Rules** turns the piece's own settings (the ones with a dot) into the pack rules. A shared setting can only go to the whole pack, so then **Apply to Pack Rules** is the only apply button.

**Markings for a whole pack.** **Pack rules → Lines and kerbs** sets the white lines, the kerbstones and their sizes for the pack. A single piece can still be changed afterwards under **Track Set → Markings**.

**Road surface texture.** Under **Track Set → Markings → Road surface texture**, a plain straight or corner can have a pattern of stones over its road, with the joints between the stones cut into the surface. Choose the texture (**Cobbles**), then the **Stone size**: **As drawn** (stones about 11 mm across), a model scale from 1:24 to 1:64, or **Custom** with a percentage of your own. **Joint width** and **Joint depth** are set apart from the stone size, so a joint stays printable when the stones are small. The joints keep clear of the guide slots, the braid and painted lines, and the road stays plain over a connector pocket. **Along the piece** decides how the pattern meets the next piece: **Same size everywhere** lays the stones at exactly the size you set and starts the pattern again at each track joint; **Fit to each piece** stretches it a little so that a whole number of repeats fits the piece and its joints line up with the next one. Small stones make a large file and take longer to build. The texture is on the road only, not on borders or special pieces. For a whole pack, **Pack rules → Road surface** has the same settings; applying writes them into every plain straight and corner, and new pieces start with them. In the Track Planner, select one or more sections and open **Road surface** to give them a texture of their own; special pieces in the selection keep a plain road, and a texture set in a layout stays when the pack changes.

**Trackside for a whole pack.** Open **Pack rules → Trackside**. The same settings as under Track Set → Trackside are there for the pack: choose the sides, the shapes and their sizes, then **Apply to all pieces**. Every piece of the pack gets them, and a piece added later starts with them, so a whole circuit comes out the same way without setting each piece. A single piece can still be changed afterwards under **Track Set → Trackside**, and a section of a layout in the Track Planner; applying the pack's trackside again sets every piece back to it.

**Guard rails.** Under **Trackside → Shape**, choose **Single guard rail** or **Double guard rail**. **Height above the road** is the top of the rail; the posts end flush with it, hidden behind the rail. A single rail hangs under that top with open space below it, and **Rail height** sets how tall it is (10 mm to start with). A double rail fills from the top to just above the road with two rails, one tight above the other. Each piece has one post near its finish end, on a tab in the edge, and its rail comes from the post of the piece before it, so a rail bridges the joint between two pieces. Switch on **Post at the start of the row** for the first piece of a row, and use **Barriers per segment** for more posts on a long piece. Two rail ends meet on a post: each has a corner notch that drops over the stem of a T-shaped peg, and the head of the T pinches both ends against the post. A post exports on its side and a rail exports flat and straight, both without support; on a bend the rail bends to follow the posts. Every size is a setting: **Rail thickness**, **Post width** (along the edge, never less than the tab width), **Post depth**, **Post top below the rail top**, **Post distance from the end** (12 mm to start with; keep it the same for all pieces of a row), the **Clearance above the road** of a double rail, and the **Pinch**, which is how much tighter the gap under the T is than the rail. The end of the last rail in a row has no neighbour to hold it, so it may want a drop of glue.

**W-beam guard rails.** **Single W-beam guard rail** and **Double W-beam guard rail** are a second kind beside the flat rails. The rail is a thin sheet folded into a W, as a real guard rail is: two crests with flat tops and sloped sides, a valley between them and a lip at the top and bottom, hollow behind. A double rail is one sheet with four crests. Set the **Profile depth** (2.7 mm to start with, the proportion of a real rail at 10 mm tall) and the **Wall thickness**. The post has a hook over the top lip of the rail and one under its bottom lip: set the bottom lip in its hook, squeeze the rail a little and press the top lip in, or slide the rail in from the side. Nothing presses on the crests. The two rail ends on a post butt without a gap, and a **cover** goes over the joint: it has the same W shape, and a tab on its back passes through the valley of both rail ends into a groove in the post, which stops the rails from sliding along. **Reach of the hooks** sets how far the hooks overlap the lips, and the **Pinch** tightens the tab of the cover in its groove. The top hook stands about 0.9 mm above the rail at each post. The post exports on its side, the rail on its lips and valleys with the crests up, and the cover on its side, all without support; a long rail touches the bed on narrow strips, so a brim helps it stay down.

**Advertising boards and catch fences.** Under **Trackside → Shape**, choose **Advertising board** for a flat wall instead of a concrete barrier: print your own sign on paper and glue it to the face. **Board thickness** starts at 2 mm, and a board end gets a rounded top corner. Under **Catch fence**, switch on **Fence on the board** to give each board its own fence: diamond mesh and horizontal cables between two square posts, a quarter and three quarters of the way along, so boards in a row give an even rhythm of posts. The posts drop 10 mm into sockets in the top of the board, and the board becomes at least thick enough for a wall on both sides of each post (5.8 mm for a 3 mm post). Set the **Fence height above the board**, **Post size**, **Cable spacing** and **Mesh size**. A fence exports flat, in one piece, and prints without support; on a bend it is still printed flat and its thin mesh bends to follow the board. A board shorter than 40 mm gets no fence.

**Borders → Border ends** gives each side independent entry and exit finishes. Choose Rounded, Tapered or S-curve, select Short or Long, and drag the tip in the border diagram or enter its distance from the start along that road edge. Set the exit on the following straight to leave the remaining road bare. The full-width section accommodates the selected clips and keepers.

**Library** stores editable pieces and their collections in SQLite on this device. Open **Library** in the top bar from any workspace. To update a piece, edit it in Track Set and choose **Library → Save current piece → Update saved piece**. Choose **New track pack** to start empty, copy an existing collection, or get a fresh copy of the starter pack. **Pack rules** applies cross-section and connector settings to the collection after checking each piece. When saving a piece with different settings you can adopt the collection profile or apply the change to the other members. **Back up SQLite** downloads the entire library for safekeeping or transfer to another browser or computer. **Save** in the top bar downloads the current project as a separate .slotfab file.

**SlotForge Basic** includes three 200 mm wide straights, 200, 100 and 50 mm long. Six 30° bends nest concentrically, with inner radii of 200, 400, 600, 800, 1,000 and 1,200 mm. The two lanes sit 100 mm apart on a 10 mm hollow road. The 3 mm wide, 8 mm deep guide slot leaves a 2 mm floor. Each braid recess is 5 mm wide and 0.5 mm deep, with a 0.6 mm wall between it and the guide slot. The starter bends use 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9 print segments on a usable 200 × 200 mm plate. Check plate fit again after changing connectors or bed margins.

**SlotForge Basic Single Lane** has the same straight lengths and 30° turns on a 100 mm road. Each slot sits 50 mm from either edge. The six inner radii run from 200 to 700 mm in 100 mm steps. For example, single-lane R1 and R2 together trace both lanes of the two-lane R1. Choose inside or outside borders, kerbs, markings and connectors in Track Set as you would for a two-lane piece.

Both Basic packs include a 200 mm long **Starting grid** straight. The 100 mm wide single-lane piece has one mark centred on its slot. Open **Road → Starting grid** to set **Mark width** across the car, **Line thickness** and **Leg length**. Zero-length legs give a transverse line. Longer legs form an open box. On multi-lane straights, **Grid positions** and **Row offset** place the marks side by side or staggered. Enable **Recess the mark outline** to cut a painting guide into the STL. Its width and depth come from **Shared line and groove dimensions**. To create another grid piece in a pack, choose **Add track piece → Straight → Starting grid**.

**Figure-eight tracks and bridge supports.** In **Add track piece → Freeform path**, choose **Flat pieces on supports** when a road crosses itself. Circuit SVG and DXF imports with identifiable crossing lane paths offer the same choice. Select which passage goes over. Under **Road → Bridge and supports**, enter the clear space above the lower running surface and the number of blocks per crossing. With approach blocks enabled, straight pieces rise and descend on graduated supports. A curved bridge deck stays level. At a change of slope the road has plain mitred ends. Integral tabs can join pieces on the same slope, and level sections accept your usual clips. The live side view shows the grade and clearance. Each support spans both road walls, and its full footprint clears the lower road, borders and hardware. If the requested blocks do not fit, the editor identifies the crossing and keeps the last valid build.

**Export STLs → Bridge supports only** downloads the numbered block STLs. Select one block in Parts to export it individually, or choose **All printable parts** for the road, blocks, assembly map and parts list. Road and border STLs from a crossing lie driving surface down, including the approach pieces; with raised lands for copper tape they lie driving surface up. The blocks print on their table-facing bases. The portable ZIP includes an editable figure eight with six blocks for a 200 × 200 mm print plate.

**STL Edit** opens with a four-post enclosure. Choose **Open STL** to start with your own model. Click a surface to see its face tools, including **Trim**, **Push / pull**, **Tilt**, **Text**, **Hollow**, and **Add/Cut profile**. Use **Surface selection** to adjust how sharply the selection can turn across adjacent triangles.

### Off-centre slots

A straight or curve with one to eight lanes can carry its guide slots, with their braids, away from their usual places. Choose **Slot position → Off-centre** in **Add track piece**, or later under **Road → Lane layout** in Track Set. Under **Measure from**, pick a road edge, then enter the distance from that edge to the centre of the slot. Typed distances settle on half millimetres. Special pieces, freeform paths and imported drawings keep their slots where they are. Bring the slots back to their usual places on a straight before a special piece.

With two or more lanes the slots are numbered from the edge you measure from: **Slot 1** is the slot nearest that edge. **Lanes** offers two ways to place them:

- **Lanes move together** asks for slot 1 at end A and end B. The other slots follow every lane spacing, further from the edge. A new lane spacing moves them, and a new road width keeps the typed distance of slot 1.
- **Set each lane** gives a small table with one row per slot and a column for end A and end B (a curve has one column). Lane spacing then plays no part in where the slots are. The slots must stay in order at both ends, slot 1 nearest the edge, and neighbouring slots must stay far enough apart for their braids. A table spaced exactly the lane spacing at both ends is stored as lanes moving together. Switching to **Set each lane** fills the table from the slots of lanes moving together; switching back keeps slot 1.

A piece cannot both squeeze or spread its lanes and move its slots. Turn off **Squeeze / spread** or set **Slot position** to **Centred**.

Every piece has an **end A**, where it starts, and an **end B**, where it finishes. On a straight, **Left edge** and **Right edge** are the sides you see looking from end A to end B. The creator preview and the Library tile mark both ends and put an arrowhead on the slot. In the preview the left edge is at the top. On a curve the choice is **Outer edge** or **Inner edge**.

On a straight, **At end A** and **At end B** can differ. The slot then crosses the road in a smooth S and leaves both ends square to the joint, so it lines up with the slot of the next piece. A longer piece makes a gentler S. A curve keeps one distance all the way round. Change position on a straight before or after it. With lanes that move together every slot follows the same S. With **Set each lane** each slot makes its own S between its two distances, so the gaps between the slots change along the piece. Slots that run at an angle come closer together than their distances at the ends suggest, so the spacing narrows mid-way; a longer piece narrows it less.

The line below the distances gives the distance of every slot from the other edge and the room on this road. A distance outside that room, slots that cross or come too close for their braids, or an S too sharp for a guide to follow stops the build with a message that names the limit. The notes underneath are advice and never stop a build. They report the tightest bend in the slots and the piece length that would make it gentle, the angle of the slots mid-way, a spacing that narrows noticeably mid-way and the length that would keep it, slots closer than the pack's lane spacing (cars on neighbouring lanes may touch), an edge so close that a car body hangs over it (a border on that side helps), and connectors that have moved.

Clips and tabs keep clear of the guide slot and the braids. With the slot near one edge they cannot sit where they do on a centred piece, so they move across the joint. Two pieces mate when their slots are the same distance from the same edge at the joint they share. Check the joint in **Connections** after moving a slot.

A piece that moves the slot to the right does so whichever way round it is placed. To bring the slot back, make a second piece with the two distances swapped. With tab joints a piece fits one way round only and cannot be turned round, so plan which end is A.

A left-hand corner in the Track Planner is a mirrored print of the corner you designed, so its slot swaps sides. A corner with the slot 35 mm from its outer edge has the slot on the left as a right-hander and on the right as a left-hander. To keep the slot on the same side of the car through a left-hand corner, make a second corner measured from the other edge.

The Track Planner joins two pieces only when their slots meet. A piece whose slot starts somewhere else is not placed, and the message gives both distances at that joint. Catalogue tiles and the parts list show each piece's slot position, such as **slot 35-145 from left**, or for several lanes every slot at end A, then at end B, such as **slots 35/135-55/155 from left**, so a piece and its mirror image read apart.

Pack rules keep the slot positions where they can:

- A new road width keeps each typed distance from its edge.
- A new lane count keeps lanes that move together: slot 1 stays where it was typed and the new lanes follow every lane spacing. A piece set lane by lane is centred again, because its table is for its old lane count.
- A new lane spacing moves the slots of lanes that move together and leaves a table set lane by lane as it is. A table whose gaps all equal the new spacing becomes lanes that move together, and from then on it follows later spacing changes; the notice after applying says how many pieces this turned.
- A piece whose slots the new rules put where a centred piece has them is centred too, for example lanes moving together 30 mm from the edge of a 200 mm road with a third lane at 70 mm spacing.

The pack rules box says how many pieces the new width, lane count or spacing centres before you apply, and the notice says so afterwards. Setting the rules back does not bring their slot positions back.

If a piece cannot take the new rules, for example because a slot would leave the road, the whole change is refused and the message names the piece.

### Planning a track layout

Open **Track Planner**, choose a pack at the top of the column on the left and click a piece to place it. The pieces are grouped as straights, corners, drawn pieces and special pieces; the specials the pack does not have yet are small **+** buttons under **Add a special piece**, made from the pack rules, and **Place the open piece** places the piece open in Track Set. The selected open end is where the next piece snaps. Under **Corners and branches turn**, choose **Left** or **Right** before placing a corner. Beside the layout's name a label tells its state: how many ends are open, **Closed loop**, or **Check the joints**; the line under the plan counts its sections, parts and road. Click a section to **Reverse turn** or **Delete** it. On the right, **This section** has its cards (bridge and approaches, barriers, lines and kerbs, road surface), each with what it holds on its header line, and **This layout** has the parts tally and the print plate. If you remove a middle section, choose an open end and use **Snap disconnected runs** to rejoin the runs.

Each placement starts with the piece’s saved cuts. The planner checks the generated parts against the current **Print plate** and adds cuts when needed. Every remaining section has its own selectable ends. **Add a special piece** includes the pit, diamond crossing, hairpin, duel, chicane and timing straight. **3D** displays the generated track parts.

Select the upper road at a crossing and open **Bridge and approaches**. Set the lift at each end in millimetres and choose a support count. Adjacent ends share the height, and straight approaches can slope. A new piece carries on from the one it joins: a straight keeps climbing or descending at the same rate, down to the table at most, a corner stays level at that height, and a lifted piece takes the same supports. Under **Support type**, choose **Full-width block** or **Two pegs**. Pegs stand in pairs, one under each road edge, so a count of 2 gives four pegs. Set **Peg width** (across the road) and **Peg length** (along the road) from 5 to 100 mm; they start at 20 × 20 mm. The top of every support follows the slope of the road above it. A road too narrow for two pegs of that width with a gap between them gets blocks. The crossings list reports the clearance above the lower track. Supports avoid lower roads and borders.

**Segmenting a layout for printing.** Every piece of a new pack starts in one road segment (for a pack made earlier, **Pack rules → Print segments → Reset every piece to one segment** makes its pieces whole again), and a piece is placed whole, with any cuts set for it in Track Set; nothing is cut to fit the print plate while you build. The line under the plan counts the parts that are over the plate. When the layout is ready, choose **Segment for printing** above the plan. A window asks whether the width, depth and margin are those of your print bed; correct them there if not, then choose **Segment layout**. Every piece that does not fit is cut into the fewest equal segments that do, and a message reports how many pieces were cut and whether any part still does not fit. A piece on a ramp, or with a section removed, keeps its own cuts. Undo brings the uncut pieces back. **Export waits for this check:** a layout exports its print kit only after it has been segmented for the print bed that is set. Placing another kind of piece, changing a piece's barriers or changing the print plate asks for the check again; choosing Export then opens the same window first.

**Markings in a layout.** Select a section and open **Lines and kerbs** to switch the white lines and the kerbs on the road and on the borders on or off for that section; Ctrl-click or Shift-click selects more sections first. Line and kerb sizes come from the piece.

**Barriers in a layout.** Select a section and open **Barriers**. The panel has the outside (left) and the inside (right) one below the other: choose a **Type** for each side, or **None**, with its ends, height and fence, then **Apply barriers**. Only that section gets them, and the plan draws them beside it. To give several sections barriers at once, Ctrl-click (or Shift-click) more sections in the plan first; they get a dashed outline and the button applies to all of them. **Sloped at end A** or **end B** runs the barrier down to track height where a row of barriers stops. Choose **None** on a side to take its barrier off again. The barriers are issued with their section in the layout's print kit.

**Save** downloads an editable `.slotplan` with the piece recipes. **Export STLs** downloads the placed sections, joining hardware, requested supports and a quantity list in `parts.csv`.

### Creating from an empty workspace

Choose **STL Edit → New design**. **Solid shape** starts a dimensioned box, cylinder, cone, sphere or tube. **Sketch** opens a drawing on the top, front or side workplane. Pick a flat face before starting a sketch to draw on the part. Set a plane offset for a parallel drawing. **Import outline** opens SVG or DXF geometry in the same sketch editor.

To move a live solid shape, drag the box or the centre handle across its workplane. Drag an **X**, **Y** or **Z** arrow for one-axis travel, or enter an exact offset under **Placement**. **Undo move** restores the preceding position. After applying the shape as a separate part, select it in **Parts** and choose **Move** below the viewport. Its arrows now use world axes.

Draw with the mouse or enter exact coordinates and dimensions. The sketch editor supports lines, three-point arcs, rectangles, circles, slots and an L-profile. Snap to the grid, centres, endpoints or the projected face. Select a saved sketch in the sidebar to see its outline and shape tools. Click its history entry to revise its dimensions.

#### Drawing relative to an imported part

1. Open the STL, click a flat face and choose **Look at face**. Open **Grid** in the viewport toolbar to change the square size directly on the model. Choose **Sketch on this grid** to start drawing.
2. Set **Square size** in millimetres. On a 235 × 117 mm face, **Equal edge margins** fits 47 × 23 whole 5 mm squares with 1 mm at the top and bottom. At 10 mm, it fits 23 × 11 squares with 2.5 mm at each side and 3.5 mm at each end. **From lower-left corner** places the leftover space at the right and top. **Exact fit** finds the largest square that divides both measured extents at the supported precision. For this face, that gives 1 mm.
3. For a drawing measured from a screw hole, choose **Set zero** and click its projected centre. Choose **Align X to edge** and click a straight edge. **Reverse X** switches the positive direction. You can now enter a point such as X = 20 mm, Y = 10 mm relative to that hole and edge. **Grid** controls visibility and **Snap** controls snapping.
4. Finish the sketch and save the project to keep its grid settings. Select the sketch and choose **Look at face** to return to its drawing orientation. Zooming out hides closely spaced lines while the snap interval keeps its specified size.

The viewport's **Top**, **Bottom**, **Left**, **Right**, **Front**, **Back** and **Isometric** buttons centre the visible selected parts. With no visible selection, they frame everything shown. **Fit all** frames every visible part from the current direction. The axis indicator shows how the world axes point on screen.

| Tool | How to use it |
| --- | --- |
| Extrude | Select a closed profile, drag its distance handle or enter a dimension. Choose **New part**, **Join** or **Cut**. |
| Revolve | Select a closed profile, place its horizontal or vertical axis and enter the angle. Use a full turn for a turned part or a partial turn for a segment. |
| Sweep | Choose a closed profile and a sketch containing one connected open path. The section follows that planar path. |
| Loft | Choose two separated section sketches. Their outer shapes can differ. For a hollow adapter draw the same number of inner openings in each section. |

To make the shelf bracket, draw an L-profile on **XZ** with 40 and 30 mm legs and a 4 mm wall. Extrude it 24 mm. Pick the broad horizontal flange, place a 3 mm hole 8 mm from the outer end and mirror the hole across the part’s X centre. Change the first sketch’s width to 70 mm in history. Both hole centres stay 8 mm from the outside ends. Save the editable project to keep these relationships and export STL for printing.

### Working with imported models

**Edit existing holes.** Click the lip of a cylindrical hole where it meets a flat face. A tooltip distinguishes a hole rim from the adjacent face. Shift-click other rims on the same part, then choose **Countersink** above the model. In the inspector, choose **Countersink**, **Counterbore**, **Resize bore** or **Fill hole**. For example, select four different-sized bores, enter a 6 mm head diameter and an 82° included angle, then apply once. Each centre and original bore stays in place beneath its seat. A failed seat is marked on the model. Resize and fill work on a continuous cylindrical section with a flat end.

**Finish a rounded corner.** Click anywhere along the curved lip of a corner to select the bend between its adjoining walls. Shift-click another corner to include it, or Shift-click a selected corner to remove it. Choose **Finish edge** and enter a round radius or chamfer distance. **Individual segment** gives you one straight span of the mesh. **Entire face perimeter** follows the picked loop, and **Tangent chain** continues through gently changing directions. Imported STLs keep their original outline. The finish follows the facets present in that file.

To update an earlier chamfer made by picking segments, open it in the project history. Set **Selection** to **Edge or rounded corner** and click **Apply**. Segments on the same bend become one selection, and any straight edges you picked stay included.

**Change a wall or remove a feature.** Select a flat wall and choose **Modify walls**. Shift-click more walls, then set an offset per side or a draft angle and fixed plane. To remove an isolated straight-sided boss or rib, click its flat top, choose **Remove feature** and check the highlighted landing. Picking the flat floor of a pocket fills it to the surrounding face.

**Stretch part of a model.** Under **Edit part**, choose **Stretch section**. Place two planes across the part, choose which end stays fixed and enter the length change. A negative value shortens the interval. Everything beyond the two planes keeps its original dimensions. Pick a flat face first to make its normal available as a stretch direction.

- **Add and combine models.** Choose **Add model** under **Parts** to bring another STL into the project. Select two parts there and choose **Align** to centre one on top of the other, match their centres, or snap two picked flat faces together. Use **Combine** to join selected parts, subtract cutters from the chosen target, or keep their shared volume. A negative placement gap lets two parts overlap before joining.
- **Inspect and repair.** A damaged STL opens in **Inspect and repair STL** before it replaces or joins your project. **Mesh tools → Inspect STL file…** opens the same view for an STL you choose. Click an outlined opening to preview a patch, correct inconsistent winding, or isolate and remove a loose component. Once the result is a closed solid, choose **Open as new model** or, when using **Add model**, **Add to project**.
- **Edit curved surfaces.** Under **Mesh tools → Brush & lasso**, paint a visible region or draw around it. Grow and shrink the selection along the mesh, then preview **Smooth** or **Remove and patch**. The selection and result appear side by side. **Protect sharp edges** helps preserve creases while smoothing.
- **Hollow and label.** Pick a flat face, choose **Hollow**, and set the wall thickness. The face becomes the opening. To add lettering, pick a flat or curved surface, choose **Text**, enter the label, and drag it into position. Raised and engraved lettering become printable geometry. Enable **Keep addition as a separate part** to print raised letters on their own.
- **Simplify a mesh.** Choose **Mesh tools → Simplify mesh**, set the allowed deviation and, if desired, a triangle target. Check the before/after triangle counts and silhouette before applying.
- **Look inside.** Turn on **Section view** in the viewport toolbar. Pick X, Y, or Z, then drag the slider or enter a position in millimetres. **Flip side** reveals the other half. The cut is for viewing. Saving and exporting use the complete model.

To adjust screw posts together, click one flat top and Shift-click the others. You can also turn on **Add to selection** and click each top. Click a highlighted top again in this mode to remove it. The tops can start at different heights on one part and must face the same direction. The editor can recognize the individual post area beside a round bore when the surrounding wall shape is identifiable. **Select whole face** switches that pick to the continuous rim.

Choose **Push / pull**. To lower posts from 10, 12, 14 and 16 mm to 8, 10, 12 and 14 mm, move each by **−2 mm**. To make them level, set a common height of **12 mm**. The 10 mm post rises, the 12 mm post stays in place and the taller posts shorten. Height is measured from the opposite extent of the selected part along the face normal. For upward-facing posts, that is the underside of the part. Check the live outlines, then apply the group as one undoable edit.

To shorten the whole enclosure, choose **Cut off top** in the sidebar. Set **Remove amount** to **3 mm** or **Finished height** to **13 mm**. The live dimensions and cut plane show how much material will remain. **Apply trim** records the cut in the editable history. For an angled cut, select a flat face and choose **Trim**. The cut runs parallel to the selected face. **Push / pull** changes material within the selected outline and can create a separately printable addition.

Use **Add profile** or **Cut profile** with a face outline, rectangle, circle, round-ended slot or closed SVG/DXF. A round-ended slot takes a tip-to-tip length and width. Set its rotation under **Angle and placement**. Choose **Cut extent → Through part** for a hole or **Fixed distance** for a pocket. **Tilt** lets you position a hinge and raise or lower a face. **Split & rebuild** (its own entry in the left column, and also **Cut → Split into pieces**) splits a part on a plane or a radial angle and copies end details onto the cut pieces, and **Transform → Size by dimension** scales a part proportionally to a target width, length, or height. Use the ruler to calibrate against a known distance.

Hold the scroll wheel and drag to pan in either workspace. Ctrl-drag and right-drag also pan. Left-drag to orbit and scroll to zoom. Use the ruler to pick two points on the same part. Click the print plate dimensions to set the usable area and edge margin. The check considers XY rotation, the whole finished footprint, and protruding connectors. For oversized generated parts, try a suggested segment count before changing the design.

**Save** writes a portable .slotfab file with embedded source geometry and edit history. Local autosave records applied changes where browser storage is available. **Apply & save** records the live edit before saving. Exporting also applies the current edit.

**Export STLs** offers the selected part, all border segments, or all printable parts. For a single border, select its segment in Assembly and choose **Export STLs → Selected part**. The borders ZIP contains one STL per border segment. The complete kit includes separate STLs, quantities, a numbered assembly map and the editable project. Under **Other STL outputs** you can export an assembled model or, in the editor, the additions or modified base. Hiding a part changes its visibility. STL dimensions are in millimetres.

### Where tools belong

Road and Borders each own the shape, surface markings and segmentation of that component. Cross-section owns the road's deck, guide slots and braid channels. Connections owns mating geometry and fit across components. Dimensions that intentionally link the road and its borders are labelled as shared. In the STL editor, commands that depend on a picked surface belong in the face toolbar. Commands that act on a whole part belong in the left navigation. Save, printing and camera controls are available across the workspaces.

## Geometry supported in this release

**Track Set → Connections → Project clip type** sets one clip family for road seams, border attachments and border ends. The **double-dovetail draw key** has a 26 × 10 mm footprint. Its tapered shoulders draw the pieces together, and one keeper locks it at the seam. The key grows with the track height, leaving a 2 mm roof above its pocket and 0.15 mm of vertical clearance. A 10 mm track takes a 4.20 mm key and a 12 mm track takes a 6.20 mm key. The minimum track height is 7.5 mm. The deck thickness setting controls the hollow deck away from the connector.

**Carrera + two retainers** takes the original 60 × 14.8 × 4.5 mm fork and needs at least 9.5 mm of track height. A complete joint has two keepers, one on each piece, 15 mm from the seam. The fork seats from underneath while the two pieces are aligned.

**Kevan Sideclip + two screws** is a flat plate with a straight outer edge, straight sides and a straight inner end with rounded inner corners: 30 mm wide, 18 mm deep and 3 mm thick, with 6 mm corners, by default. Each screw hole keeps at least 4 mm of clip to every edge and corner. It slides into a pocket from the side face of the road, across the seam, until its straight edge is flush with the side. One countersunk wood screw in each piece cuts its thread into a pilot-size hole in the clip and on into a pilot hole in the road beyond it, which holds the clip tight. The clips sit at both road edges of every road joint with clips; switch either side off for a piece under **Road connections** (switching off both is refused). The screws go in from the top, from the bottom, or per road side. The pocket sits halfway up the road, so either way a screw grips equally: on a 12 mm road a 2.5 × 10 mm screw has 3 mm of thread in the clip and 2.35 mm in the road. Leave **Screw length** empty to use the longest common length that fits the road height: 10 mm on a 12 mm road, 8 mm on a 10 mm road. The clip settings are part of the pack's print profile. Kevan Sideclips join road ends only: attach and join borders with tabs, and fit the clip before a border on that side, which covers the side face.

**Connections → Tab and socket** sets tab width, projection from the seam and thickness. Leave thickness empty to follow track height. The socket updates live, adding the gap on both sides, above and below, and once at the tip. For example, an 8 × 7 × 3 mm tab with a 0.15 mm gap gives a socket 8.30 mm wide, 7.15 mm deep and 3.30 mm high.

Under **Track Set → Connections**, choose a joint type and the number of clips or tabs per joint. One goes in the middle, two form an outer pair, and three add a centre connector to that pair. **Tabs and clips** lets you choose each count, up to five total. Three clips and two tabs alternate across the joint, with a clip in the middle and at either end. Choosing three tabs and two clips reverses that pattern. Foundry checks the available space and shows the result live. **Apply changes** keeps the layout.

Road ends can follow the segment seams or use their own settings. On borders, choose how many connectors attach each segment to the road and how many join neighbouring border ends. For an exact location, open the placement map and drag a mark or enter its position. Choosing an automatic count recalculates the row.

When a connector cannot fit, **View warning** opens the joint’s diagnosis in the inspector. For a narrow border end, it shows the available width, the minimum for the selected connector and a suggested width. The edit button opens the relevant setting and keeps the rest of the draft. The viewport shows the last applied model until the edited joint fits.

**Export fit samples** makes two mating racks, a tab, the main clip, the keepers for one joint and a reusable keeper tool. The racks let you try three lateral clearances with your track filament and print settings. The same angled tool fits the dovetail and Carrera keepers. Download it separately under **Connections → Project clip type → Download keeper tool STL**, or find it in a print kit containing keepers.

Support the track with about 6 mm of space above the table to slide the bare tool underneath. Allow about 8 mm to carry a loose keeper on its tip. Align the track pieces, press the main clip into its underside pocket and insert each keeper with its wings across the seam. Engage the slotted centre, press toward the road surface and turn clockwise a quarter turn as viewed from underneath. To release, press inward about 0.5 mm, turn counter-clockwise a quarter turn and lower the keeper. The seated keeper clears the tabletop by 0.30 mm.

Under **Cross-section → Braid recess**, **Braid offset from slot** sets the width of the road-surface wall between each edge of the guide slot and the adjacent braid recess. A 2 mm offset leaves a 2 mm wall on each side. At 0 mm each recess meets the slot directly. **Width per strip** sets the width of each recess independently. The feed slit follows its recess, and the slot and recesses open through each segment end. Under **Braid pass-throughs**, **Opening length** sets the length of the slit along the lane.

**Braid style** chooses where each strip sits. **Recessed** cuts a recess of the given **Depth** for braid. **Raised land for copper tape** leaves the deck whole and builds a flat land of the same width that stands the **Raised height** (0.05 to 0.8 mm, 0.2 mm by default) above the road, so adhesive-backed copper tape sits slightly proud of the driving surface. The lands stop at the guide slot, at every braid pass-through and at the insulating gaps of a hairpin or diamond. Depth plays no part while the style is raised. The braid style is a pack rule, like the other braid dimensions. A piece with raised lands always exports driving-face up, also as a special piece, a flat crossing or a Track Planner print, because the lands cannot lie on the print bed. Pit lanes and timing straights are made for recessed braid and ask you to choose **Recessed** for that piece.

The double-dovetail key and quarter-turn keeper are original SlotForge Foundry designs. Under **Road → Road white lines** and **Borders → Border white line**, choose the inside, outside, or both sides independently. Each piece carries the full stripe where it runs alone. Where both carry it, the line straddles their shared edge. For example, a 4 mm line has 2 mm on the road and 2 mm on its border. Use matching width and side settings on adjoining track pieces to continue the line through the joint.

Choose **Centre line → Solid** or **Dashed** for a marking along the middle of the road. **Dash length** and **Gap between dashes** apply to the centre line and dashed pit crossings. Their pattern continues through the print seams of a piece. Slots and braid recesses interrupt the paint wherever they occupy the road centre. Use a pack’s **Pack rules → Lines and kerbs → Centre line** to apply a centre line and dash dimensions across its pieces.

Under **Borders → Border width**, each side has its own apex. Drag the outside or inside apex handle along the road to set its position, or away from the road to increase its width. You can also enter each width and percentage directly. Both sides share the entry and exit widths. Projects saved with one apex use its values on both sides until you edit a side.

Under **Road → Road kerbstones**, choose either road edge or both to place alternating red and cream blocks directly on the driving surface. They sit just inside any edge stripe and can be used on a road without borders. Under **Borders → Kerbstones**, choose the road-facing edge, free edge, or both on the active borders. Road and border blocks share their width, length and outline groove setting. The alternating colours continue across segment seams.

For hand painting, enable the recess under **Borders → Kerbstones** and set the groove width and depth there. This cuts the outline of each block into its border STL, including the divide between colours. White lines have their own recess switches. Where a road and border share a stripe, the Road switch controls the groove on its driving side. Groove dimensions are shared with other enabled paint recesses. To print all border segments, choose **Export STLs → Borders only** for a ZIP of individual STLs, each centred on the print bed.

- Generated flat straights and circular bends can include hollow decks, guide slots, braid recesses, braid pass-throughs, tapered borders, independent road and border seams, and configurable tabs, sockets and loose clips. Test joints use the selected connector dimensions and clearance.
- For Circuit imports, export the slot centrelines and road surface edges. A single open centreline can use a supplied road width. Full-size DXF uses millimetres. The SVG importer understands Circuit's compact-scale metadata. Confirm the imported dimensions, especially for third-party drawings.
- A flat panel without identifiable road ends can become a single deck. For a bridge, the importer needs continuous lane paths to follow each pass. A four-way junction made of unpaired strokes needs an ordered path for each through road. Parametric end connectors, borders and automatic segmentation require identifiable ends or a recovered crossing route. Guide-slot width and braid width are shared profile dimensions. Bridge approaches use straight planar sections. Freeform vertical profiles, banking and lane-changer mechanisms are outside this release.
- STL edits operate on closed, consistently oriented geometry. The importer welds matching vertices and reports invalid meshes. It does not reconstruct arbitrarily damaged meshes. Surface grouping crosses adjacent triangles up to the selected angle; a curved selection accepts artwork in a tangent plane. Planar-face operations require a planar selection. Straight rebuilds need matching cross-sections. Radial STL rebuilds use the given XY centre and a sector of up to 180°.
- Plate checks cover geometric fit in the chosen print orientation. Slicer supports, print settings, electrical contact and printed joint fit still need verification on the actual build. A short test joint is included for that purpose.

## Development

Use Node.js 24 and npm. Run `npm ci`, then `npm run dev` for a local development server. Run `npm test` for geometry, drawing-import and regression tests. Run `npm run build` to create the standalone HTML and portable ZIP. Third-party notices are embedded in the HTML and included in the ZIP.

`npm run test:ui` runs the production HTML with networking disabled in a real Chromium browser. It can use installed Edge on Windows or Chrome/Chromium on macOS and Linux. Set `SLOT_FORGE_BROWSER` to select an executable. Set `SLOT_FORGE_RENDERER` to `webgl` or `software` to exercise a specific renderer. CI is configured to build and run both paths on all three operating systems.
