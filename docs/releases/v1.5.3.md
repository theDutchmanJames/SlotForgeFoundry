Cobbles cut into the road, and sloped pieces that take a track smoothly up a hill and down again.

## What's new

- **Road surface texture:** a pattern of cobbles over the road of a straight or corner, with the joints between the stones cut into the surface.
- **Your size:** stones as drawn, for a scale from 1:24 to 1:64, or any size of your own; joint width and depth are set apart from it.
- **For a pack or a layout:** set a texture once in the Pack rules, or give it to selected sections in the Track Planner.
- **Sloped pieces:** a straight that bends in height, with a climb angle and a setting for how gentle or sharp the curve is.
- **A whole hill:** four forms take the road up and down again, and the straights between them follow the climb in the Track Planner.

## Details

**Road surface texture.** Under **Track Set → Markings → Road surface texture**, choose **Cobbles**. The stones are laid along and across the road, so they bend with a corner, and every copy of a piece is the same print.

- **Stone size**: **As drawn** (stones about 11 mm across), a model scale from 1:24 to 1:64, or **Custom** with a percentage.
- **Joint width** and **Joint depth**: 0.6 mm and 0.5 mm to start with. They do not change with the stone size, so a joint stays printable when the stones are small.
- **Along the piece**: **Same size everywhere** lays the stones at exactly the size you set, and the pattern starts again at each track joint. **Fit to each piece** stretches it a little so that a whole number of repeats fits the piece and its joints line up with the next piece.

The joints keep clear of the guide slots, the braid and painted lines, and the road stays plain over a connector pocket. The texture is on the road of plain straights and corners; borders and special pieces keep a plain surface. Small stones make large files: a 200 mm straight at 1:32 has about 57,000 triangles, and at 1:64 about 200,000.

**Pack rules → Road surface texture** has the same settings for a whole pack, with **Apply to all straights and corners**. In the Track Planner, select one or more sections and open **Road surface texture**.

**Sloped pieces.** Under **Track Set → Shape → Slope** a plain straight can bend in height:

| Form | Where it goes | From end A to end B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level into a climb | foot of a hill, going up | rises |
| Climb back to level | top of a hill, going up | rises |
| Level into a descent | top of a hill, going down | drops |
| Descent back to level | foot of a hill, going down | drops |

- **Climb angle**: the slope at the sloped end, from 1° to 30°.
- **Curve**: how much of the piece the bend takes. At 100 % the road bends gently over its whole length; a smaller value bends more sharply and then runs straight at the climb angle. A hint gives the rise, the run and the radius of the bend.
- **Everything else as on a level straight.** The piece is made flat and then bent, so the slots keep their depth and the braid, the connectors, the borders and a texture follow the curve.
- **Solid base.** The foot of a climb or of a descent stands on the table as one solid block. The base stops short of the high end, so connectors there can be fitted from below.

The road has to bend on a radius of at least 150 mm; a tighter bend is refused with the curve or the angle that works. A sloped piece prints on its side, so the curve of the road comes out smooth.

**A hill in the Track Planner.** A sloped piece is as long as its run on the plan. The piece behind it joins at the height it reached, and a straight behind a sloped end is tilted to the climb angle and gets a support, as behind any ramp. A descent needs the road it joins to be at least as high as it comes down; the message says how high.

The [user guide](https://github.com/theDutchmanJames/SlotForgeFoundry/blob/v1.5.3/docs/GUIDE.md) explains every setting.

## Not yet printed

The road surface texture and the sloped pieces are new and have not been printed and tried on a track yet. Print one textured straight and one sloped piece first.

- Whether a 0.6 mm joint comes out cleanly, and at which stone sizes, depends on your printer and on which way up the road is printed.
- The 150 mm radius for a sloped piece is an estimate of what a car's guide and chassis can follow, not a tested figure.
- A sloped piece is one road segment, and cannot have trackside accessories, white lines or kerbstones yet.
- Supports under a sloped piece that is itself lifted, as the top of a hill is, come out level and so too short at its high end. Check them before printing.
- In Track Set the guide lines and the lane preview of a sloped piece are drawn flat.
