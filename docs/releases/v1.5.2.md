Pit lanes you build yourself from turnouts, with a motor that fits under the road, and guard rails with a real W profile.

## What's new

- **Turnout:** a single-lane piece with three ends and four swappable inserts: Digital, Hand-set point, Analog and Closed.
- **Motor under the road:** a slider between the two slots works the blade, on a turnout and on a pit lane. No wire to bend, strokes of 2 to 12 mm.
- **Pit lanes in the Track Planner:** place turnouts left or right, build on the branch, and let the Pit ladder lay out a row of bays in one step.
- **Helpers:** a return curve for a branch, a straight made to fit a gap, and end stops for dead-end lanes.
- **W-beam guard rails:** a folded sheet that snaps into hooks on its posts, with a cover over each joint.

## Details

**Turnout.** A pack with one lane has a **Turnout** on the Special pieces shelf. One lane comes in; a straight road and a curved branch go out. The area around the split is a separate insert that goes in from below with two screws, so the braid stays where it is when you change it:

| Insert | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Digital | a moving blade, worked by a motor |
| Hand-set point | the same blade, set by hand; it clicks into either position |
| Analog | no moving parts; both slots stay open |
| Closed | the branch slot is filled in, level with the road |

The road is the same for all four. A turnout prints as one road piece without borders, trackside accessories or road markings.

**The motor drive.** A stout slider lies between the two slots and moves along the road. A pin on the blade's crank stands in a slanted slot in the slider, with a flat stretch at each end, and a push-pull motor lies in line behind it. Nothing passes under a guide slot. Enter the travel of your motor as **Usable stroke**, from 2 to 12 mm; from about 5 mm the slant is shallow and the flat ends hold the blade in place.

**Pit lanes use the same drive.** With **Push-pull motor under the road** on, a pit lane's flipper is worked by the same slider, between the main slot and the pit slot. The motor cradle at the pit-side shoulder and its bent pushrod under the pit slot are gone. A pit lane saved with a motor is rebuilt with the new drive when it opens: check its motor measurements, and print the slider, the crank and the cradle again. Without the motor, the crank keeps its eye for a pushrod of your own.

**Turnouts in the Track Planner.** A single-lane pack lists the Turnout under **Specials**. Choose **Left** or **Right** for the side of the branch. The turnout joins with its entry, building carries on from its main exit (end B), and the branch (end C) stays open to build on. Select a placed turnout to choose its insert; nothing moves in the layout when you change it. A turnout is never cut by **Segment for printing**.

**Pit lane helpers.**

- **Splits the lane / Joins two lanes** sets which way round a turnout goes in. A joining turnout takes the pit lane back onto the main road.
- **Add return curve** turns the open branch of the selected turnout back parallel to the main road, with corners of the pack.
- **Fit a straight** makes a straight of exactly the length of the gap between the build end and the open end that faces it.
- **Pit ladder** builds a row of bays from the chosen end: a turnout, a bay and an end stop each, parallel to the main road or at the branch angle. It suggests the least spacing that keeps the bays apart, and one Undo takes the whole ladder away.

**End stops.** An end stop is a straight whose slots and braid stop short of a closed end without connectors. It is under **Specials** in the Track Planner, and a switch under **Shape** on any plain straight in Track Set.

**W-beam guard rails.** **Single W-beam guard rail** and **Double W-beam guard rail** are two more Trackside shapes, beside the flat guard rails. The rail is a thin sheet folded into a W. It snaps between a hook over its top lip and one under its bottom lip, so nothing presses on its crests, and a cover over each joint passes a tab through both rail ends into the post.

**Fixes.**

- Where both pieces of a joint bring clips for it, the parts tally of a layout counts one set.
- The parts of a turnout and a pit lane are listed as Turnout blades, Turnout cranks and Turnout pivot bearings.

The [user guide](https://github.com/theDutchmanJames/SlotForgeFoundry/blob/v1.5.2/docs/GUIDE.md) explains every setting.

## Not yet printed

The turnout, its four inserts, the slider drive and the W-beam guard rails are new and have not been printed and tried on a track yet. Print one turnout and one length of rail first, and check the fit of the insert, the slider and the cover before printing a whole pit lane.

- Joints between two ends of the same kind, as at a joining turnout, need clips. Integral tabs carry the same half on both ends there, and the Track Planner says so.
- The motor leads drop through a notch behind the motor, so the baseboard needs a hole there.

## Verification

889 unit tests passed on Windows. The browser stages clicked through the shipped app with networking disabled; they now build a pit lane from two turnouts with return curves and a fitted straight, a pit ladder of three bays, and a pit lane with its motor under the road.

The macOS and Linux downloads are built and tested by GitHub Actions after this release is published, on each operating system.

| System | Installer | Portable app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Windows | `SlotForgeFoundry-1.5.2-Setup-x64.exe` | `SlotForgeFoundry-1.5.2-Portable-x64.exe` |

Foundry is not code-signed. On Windows, choose **More info → Run anyway**. On macOS, use **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway** the first time. On Linux, mark the AppImage as executable (`chmod +x`). The SHA256SUMS files list each download's checksum.

Your Library, layouts and settings carry over from earlier versions.
